# CAREER_SUPPLY_DEMAND_MAP

**Purpose.** The decision document for Layer 3: what the combination of employer demand (Layer 1, `CAREER_DECISION_MAP.md`) and observed candidate supply (Layer 2, `docs/supply-findings.md`) says that neither side says alone. Every statement is traced to a table in `outputs/tables/` (C*, DS*, SQ*, O*, T*). It is market analysis; the profile-specific reading is separate (`docs/profile-specific-demand-supply-analysis.md`).

**Vintages.** Demand: open core data postings, Austria, 2026-09-16 (720; 719 with description; Styria 58 incl. multi-site ads, 54 by primary state). Supply: public GitHub accounts with an Austrian location signal, 2026-09-17/18 (9,409 accounts; 1,818 with a data signal = P_data; 872 with a bio-declared core data role = P_T1; 6,677 projects, 3,959 documented, 2,605 substantive); Stack Overflow 2025 (410 Austrian respondents); Eurostat graduates/occupations. **LinkedIn members were not collected** (terms; private slot for hand-coded or licensed records, D-026, still empty; LinkedIn job listings are Layer 1).

**Reading rules.** "Demand share" = share of ads that *mention* an item. "Supply share" = share of observed GitHub data-signal candidates with evidence; "project-demonstrated" = evidence in a documented, non-educational project. The two are different universes: compare rankings and orders of magnitude, never exact percentages. A low supply share for a tool that leaves no code trace (Power BI, Excel, SAP) mostly measures GitHub's blindness. Styria is the only complete supply frame; Vienna figures are lower bounds. Nothing here measures hiring. Tools are quoted at two levels: **skill level** (one vocabulary item; `T05`/`DS03`; §2, §4) and **capability bucket** (union of member skills; `DS10`; §3, §5, §6) — hence cloud 29 % vs 35 % and SQL 40.5 % vs 41 % of ads. Styrian counts follow D-027: location-resolved (56 bio-declared, 248 data-signal) unless labelled frame-B complete-Styria subset (52, 247).

## 1. Where is observable candidate supply concentrated? (C04, C05, C10, C23)

In **self-described data scientists** (73 % of bio-declared accounts; 486 "Data Scientist" + 140 "ML/AI Engineer" bios) doing **Python-notebook work** (Python 74 %, Jupyter 54 % of P_data), in **Vienna** (802 of 1,818 data-signal accounts; Styria 248 by resolved location, 247 in the frame-B complete-Styria subset), among **students and unlabelled profiles** (17 % students, 68 % no seniority word), and in **ML/NLP/LLM/CV project topics** (NLP 14 %, classification 12.5 %, LLM apps 10 %, CV 8 % of projects). Three of eight positioning clusters are pure ML/AI builders (LLM apps 175, deep-learning/CV 191, classification pipelines 133).

## 2. Where is employer demand concentrated? (T02, T03, T05)

In **data engineering and data science (22 % each), business analysis (17 %), data analytics (15 %), BI (12 %)**, in **Vienna (48 %) and Upper Austria (17 %)**, around **SQL (40 %), visualisation/dashboards (38 %), Python (35 %), data quality/governance (31 %), cloud (29 %), ETL (22 %), Azure (19 %), Power BI (18 %), Excel (18 %), data modelling (16 %)** (skill level, T05), with stakeholder/communication wording in 55–63 % of ads and German requirements stated in 40 %.

## 3. Where do they overlap? (DS01, DS03, DS10 quadrant A)

Python, data visualisation, machine learning, GenAI/LLM, statistics, ETL, CI/CD & tests, cloud (any), marketing/finance/operations domain wording: demanded by ≥ 13 % of ads and evidenced by ≥ 10 % of candidates (DS10 quadrant A, medians as thresholds). SQL (capability bucket) is in this quadrant by "any evidence" (19 %) but not by project evidence (10 %). Family-level overlap: **data analytics** is the one family where bio-declared supply and advertised demand are of the same order (129 bios vs 107 ads, relative representation 1.0); data science overlaps in kind but not in proportion.

## 4. Where do they diverge? (DS01, DS02, DS03)

* **Data science: 3.3× over-represented on the supply side** as a ratio of shares (73 % of bios vs 22 % of ads; in counts 638 bios vs 158 ads = 4.0 declared candidates per posting, the BQ20 measure; Styria 38 bios vs 13 ads = 2.9 per posting; Vienna ≥ 4.4 per posting). "Data Scientist" alone: 486 bios vs 79 ads (5×).
* **Data engineering, BI, business analysis, governance, marketing analytics: 0.07–0.30× represented** on GitHub (engineering 58 bios vs 160 ads; BI 15 vs 85; business analysis 21 vs 120; governance 7 vs 65; marketing analytics 2 vs 24). Titles with zero GitHub bios: BI Analyst (32 ads), BI Consultant (20), Data Architect (21), Marketing Data Scientist (2).
* Tools (skill level, DS03): SQL 40.5 % of ads vs 16 % of candidates (9 % project-demonstrated); Power BI 18 % vs 3 %; Excel 18 % vs 4 %; Azure 19 % vs 5 %; data modelling 16 % vs 1 %; Databricks 12 % vs 1 %. The reverse divergence: Jupyter 4 % of ads vs 54 % of candidates; JavaScript 4 % vs 42 %; PyTorch 4.5 % vs 18 %; Docker 6 % vs 21 %; R 7 % vs 19 %.

## 8. Which capabilities are rare but relevant? (DS10 quadrant D with non-trivial demand; C18)

Experimentation/A-B testing (ads 3.1 %, 22 ads; candidates 8.5 % any, **2 % project**; 12 marketing projects show it), causal inference (0.7 % / 2.6 % / 1.4 %), forecasting with a business decision (6 % / 11 % / 7 %), product analytics (2.6 % / 5 % / 3 %), dbt/analytics engineering (3.5 % / 1 %; 5 ads, 3 bios), Airflow/orchestration (2.2 % / 2.6 % / 1.8 %). ## 9. Where does language materially change the addressable market? (T07e, DS06, DS06b)

On the demand side only: Styria 8 of 58 ads English-written without a stated German requirement (Graz area 6 of 52), Vienna 86 of 349, Austria 141 of 719; by family, data science 55 of 157, engineering 29 of 160, analytics 20 of 107, BI 7 of 85. On the supply side GitHub is silent: 99 % of bios and 96 % of README sets are English regardless of the author's German. The intersection "English-only candidates competing for English-only Styrian ads" cannot be computed from public code; the manual LinkedIn slot (languages with stated levels) is the only way to fill it.

## 11. How does seniority alter the picture? (DS07, DS07b)

Ads: 32 % senior or lead wording, 57.5 % unlabelled, 10 % intern/junior. Bios: 10 % senior or lead, 68 % unlabelled, **17 % students**, 4.5 % junior. The visible GitHub supply is junior-skewed relative to advertised seniority, and the senior supply that exists rarely publishes (leads: median 0.5 projects). Experience years are not observable, so a title-to-experience mismatch cannot be tested. ## 12. How does education alter the picture? (DS08, O01)

Eight descriptive clusters, of which the largest holds 457 of 1,818 candidates with a median of 0 projects; the notebook-ML and deep-learning clusters are the dense ones, while clusters defined by pipelines, dashboards or business domains are both smaller and better evidenced - the space is chosen by what the projects show, not by the title claimed.

* **Market convergence.** Python, visualisation, ML and GenAI are the shared language of ads and public portfolios; they establish membership, not distinction.
* **Market concentration.** Both sides concentrate on Vienna; supply concentrates on the *data scientist* label and notebook ML, demand on *data engineer / business analyst / BI* titles and on SQL-centred tooling — the largest structural mismatch in the data.
* **Candidate evidence gaps.** SQL, data quality/validation, dimensional modelling/warehousing, Azure/Databricks pipelines, and business-framed analysis (context, result, limitation) are demanded far more often than they are publicly demonstrated. Power BI/Excel/SAP are also demanded and invisible, but they cannot be shown as code — screenshots, models and DAX in a repo are the workaround.
* **Candidate concentration.** Deep learning, NLP/CV, LLM demos, generic classification notebooks and course repositories are abundant relative to demand; a portfolio built only of these competes with the densest part of the pool.
* **Positioning spaces.** The combinations the data supports as *rare-but-demanded*: SQL + modelled warehouse + Power BI/dashboard with a business question (analytics/BI space; 0.6 candidates per Styrian posting); Python ETL with tests/CI into a warehouse (engineering space; 0.2 per Styrian posting); domain-framed decision analysis (experimentation, forecasting, causal) on customer/marketing/finance data (fewer than 20 public accounts show marketing + experimentation). Seniority wording and business vocabulary are themselves rare in the pool.
* **Demonstration opportunities.** In order of demand and rarity: a SQL-first project with a documented data model; a data-quality/validation layer with tests; an Azure or Databricks pipeline (even small); a README with question, business context, result and limitations; a dashboard with screenshots and the model behind it; an experimentation/causal readout on business data.
* **Transition patterns.** Public GitHub does not narrate career changes (3.4 % explicit; marketing 0). Transitioners are invisible here; the LinkedIn slot is where that evidence would live.

**Q. How common is a career transition into data - and does anyone arrive from marketing?**

Rare in public evidence and almost absent from marketing: 12.2% of bio-declared accounts name a business background, while marketing is named by 10 of 872 - but GitHub does not narrate careers (3.4 % state a transition at all), so this is evidence of silence, not evidence of absence, and the transition story is one the CV and LinkedIn must carry because the code cannot.

* **Geographic implications.** Styria has the same supply shape as Vienna at one-fifth the size; its demand is engineering/industrial, its public supply is academic/data-science; Graz is where the engineering-flavoured analytics evidence is scarcest relative to ads.
* **Language implications.** Unresolved on the supply side; the demand side alone says German-required ads are the majority in Styria (28 of 58 state a requirement).
* **Portfolio implications.** Common: notebooks, ML classifiers, tutorials, install/usage READMEs. Uncommon and demand-mapped: SQL, modelling, pipelines with tests, dashboards, business-framed results. Two or three substantive, documented projects already place an account above the median (median 1 for bio-declared accounts, 62 % with ≥ 1 substantive).
* **Career-system implications.** Monitor over time (docs/supply-methodology.md §8): T1 family mix (does data engineering/BI appear on GitHub?), the SQL/modelling evidence share, LLM-project saturation (10 % of projects now), Styrian data-signal count (248 by resolved location; 247 in the frame-B complete-Styria subset), English-only Styrian ads (8 of 58), and, once filled, the LinkedIn slot's language-level distribution.
